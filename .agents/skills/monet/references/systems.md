# Systems profile

Use this profile for generative, geometric, plotter-like, modular, architectural, or high-density work.

Treat the composition as a hierarchy of visual modules rather than an accumulation of unrelated shapes. Define reusable units in `<symbol>` or `<pattern>` elements, then deploy them through `<use>` and grouped transforms.

- Texture modules can combine 20–50 jittered strokes into a reusable etched or charcoal-like mark.
- Tiled grids, nested symbols, and repeated cells should support large compositional planes rather than replace them.
- Slightly offset or rotated line systems can create moiré and optical vibration; use enough local contrast for the effect to survive rendering.
- Organic growth units can combine curves and sparks into a reusable vector brush for foliage, currents, or networks.
- Atmospheric modules can group blurred polygons or caustic forms into a consistent lighting system.
- Make dense elements larger and higher-contrast than intuition suggests; sub-pixel detail often becomes grey noise.
- Concentrate the highest structural density near focal areas and allow negative space elsewhere.
- Break perfect tiling with controlled variation in scale, rotation, spacing, or opacity.

Form comes before system. A modular technique is successful only when the resulting structure remains legible at viewing distance.
